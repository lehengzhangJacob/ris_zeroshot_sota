\section{Conclusion}

Training-free referring image segmentation has been optimised under the
assumption that the proposal pool is the limiting factor. We tested that
assumption by decomposing the oracle gap --- the IoU that the pool makes
available minus the IoU the selector returns --- into attributable error
classes, on the full RefCOCO, RefCOCO$+$ and RefCOCOg validation splits, at two
operating points of the released HybridGL pipeline on RefCOCO, and at two
proposal-pool densities.

The pool is not the bottleneck. \emph{Wrong-instance} selection, in which the
pipeline returns a different object of the correct category, carries 77--86\,\%
of all recoverable IoU on all three benchmarks, while
under-segmentation --- the granularity class that mask-merging and
mask-refinement remedies address --- carries 5--23\,\%. Doubling the proposal
density confirms the reading from the other direction: it buys $+1.0$ mIoU of
oracle and costs the released pipeline $3.2$ mIoU, because more proposals means
more distractors to select among.

The mechanism is structural rather than incidental. The released guidance
computes a spatial-relationship term and a coherence term, but evaluates them
only on the three highest-scoring proposals under the full expression, so its
answer cannot leave that set; on wrong-instance failures the referent is
outside it 69.8\,\% of the time. A perfect chooser restricted to the set the
pipeline already computes would gain $+13.3$ mIoU, and a lexical edit of the
prompt --- removing the spatial vocabulary, which turns the expression into a
category probe --- moves the referent into the top-8 for 70.1\,\% of those
failures.

Acting on that, a training-free operator that enlarges the candidate set and
applies the pipeline's own coherence score subject to the parsed relation
raises the released pipeline to 0.5029 mIoU and 0.4431 oIoU on full RefCOCO val
($+0.0546$ mIoU, 95\,\% CI $[+0.0493,+0.0601]$), with the gain concentrated
exactly on spatial expressions and zero on the 42.6\,\% of expressions it
cannot parse.

We have been deliberately explicit about what this does not establish. The
mIoU gain is statistically indistinguishable from a one-line containment snap;
the operator is neutral on RefCOCOg and RefCOCO$+$; no multimodal chooser
baseline was run and that remains the largest open flank, bounded at $+0.227$
mIoU by the candidate-set ceiling; and absolute rows are not comparable to the
published table because our reproduction of the baseline is $5.09$ mIoU below
it on the base row, a gap we localise to the frozen feature path.

The durable result is the diagnosis. It reframes what a training-free RIS
method should be measured against: not the oracle utilisation of its pool, but
the share of the pool's recoverable IoU that its selector can actually reach.
On the evidence here, that share is around half, and the missing half is
overwhelmingly a matter of telling one instance from another.

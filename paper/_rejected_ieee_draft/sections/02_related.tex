\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Training-free referring image segmentation}

Zero-shot RIS was established by Global-Local CLIP \cite{yu2023global}, which
scores mask proposals with CLIP and fuses local (masked-region) and global
(context) representations. Ref-Diff \cite{kim2023refdiff} uses diffusion
attention with SAM; TAS \cite{li2023tas} adds BLIP-2 captions and negative
prompts; LGD \cite{li2025lgd} obtains attribute and surround descriptions from
LLaVA; GLOD-RIS \cite{wu2026glodris} adds orthogonal decoupling of the
foreground and background CLIP directions and a grid localization prompt to an
external multimodal model. HybridGL \cite{liu2025hybridgl}, our baseline,
fuses global features into the local branch (G2L) and adds three guidance
modules --- spatial relationship, GEM attention coherence, and a position
prior.

Two properties are common to all of these. First, the final answer is an
argmax over one candidate mask. Second, the weights that combine sub-scores are
global constants rather than functions of the candidate pool being scored. No
published method in this family calibrates its score against the other
candidates of the image it is scoring. Our diagnosis is about precisely that
stage, and our operator is the first, to our knowledge, to report a
pool-level decomposition of where the discarded IoU goes.

\subsection{Diagnostic and oracle analyses}

Oracle and ceiling analyses are standard in proposal-based detection
\cite{ren2015faster,he2017maskrcnn} but have not been applied to training-free
RIS. The word \emph{oracle} does not appear in the recent multimodal referring
segmentation survey, nor in the GRES/gRefCOCO \cite{liu2023gres} or GREx
\cite{liu2025grex} benchmarks, which instead document that no training-free
method has been evaluated on their generalized setting. What the field reports
is oracle utilisation --- achieved IoU over best-available IoU --- which
aggregates pool quality and selection quality into one number and therefore
cannot say which is binding. We separate the two, and find the selector
binding.

\subsection{Spatial and relational reasoning in RIS}

Spatial language is a long-standing sub-problem of referring expression
comprehension and segmentation: modular spatial relations
\cite{hu2016natural}, speaker-listener-reinforcer architectures
\cite{yu2017slr}, and, in the training-free regime, keyword-based proposal
pruning in ProposalCLIP \cite{zhou2022proposalclip} and the relationship
module of HybridGL itself. ProposalCLIP is the closest prior art on the text
side: it also removes or exploits locative words when scoring proposals, but it
prunes with hand-specified colour, position and direction heuristics on top of
a class-name-driven score and does not diagnose selection failure. HybridGL's
relationship module is closer to ours structurally, and our experiments treat
it as the baseline rather than as an alternative: our operator acts on top of
the released pipeline at its own operating point, and we report the
decomposition of the gain between candidate-set enlargement and the relation
gate (§\ref{sec:controls}).

\subsection{Mask merging and granularity remedies}

A separate family targets SAM over- and under-segmentation by merging masks:
E-SAM \cite{esam2025} merges adjacent similar SAM masks, GF-SAM
\cite{gfsam2024} groups masks by connectivity under coverage gating, and
ProposalCLIP merges proposals by IoU and CLIP similarity. MARS
\cite{mars2026} gates proposals within a tolerance of the maximum score and
unions them. These target the granularity class that our decomposition measures
at 2--18\,\% of the recoverable IoU. We include one such operator --- a
containment snap --- as a required baseline, and find that it and our selector
are complementary rather than competing.

\subsection{Selection over proposal sets}

Selecting an arbitrary subset of frozen proposals has been studied as a learned
problem (RAS \cite{ras2025} trains a selection head on roughly two million mask
groups with a fine-tuned LLaVA). We work in the training-free regime by
construction: the operator we report adds no parameters and no forward pass,
and its inputs are quantities the baseline pipeline already computes.

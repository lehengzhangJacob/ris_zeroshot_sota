\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state the limits of this work in the order in which they bound its claims.

\textbf{No multimodal chooser baseline.} The most obvious alternative to a
hand-written relation resolver is to hand the candidate set to an
off-the-shelf multimodal model and ask it to pick. We did not run that
baseline. The local reproduction of GLOD-RIS, whose pipeline depends on such a
model, broke in production (aborted at 223 of 10{,}834 expressions with a
malformed structured response), and no local vision--language weights are
available in this environment. We bound the question instead
(§\ref{sec:experiments}): any chooser acting on the operator's candidate set
could add at most $+0.227$ mIoU, and the internal alternatives we did test ---
ranking by the semantic score, and picking the geometric extremum --- both lose
to the coherence ranking. This is the largest open flank of the paper and we
flag it as such rather than reporting the ceiling as if it were the comparison.

\textbf{The mIoU gain is not distinguishable from a one-line baseline.} On
full RefCOCO val the operator's mIoU delta is statistically indistinguishable
from a containment snap ($+0.0005$, $p=0.87$). The selector separates on oIoU
($+3.6$ points), on part expressions, and on coverage of the GT-area quartiles,
and the two compose almost additively. A reader who cares only about mIoU on
this benchmark should read the two rows as tied.

\textbf{The repair does not transfer uniformly.} It is neutral on RefCOCOg
($-0.0034$) and RefCOCO$+$ ($+0.0012$). On RefCOCO$+$ the null is expected ---
1.6\,\% of expressions are spatial --- and the residual spatial stratum is
positive. On RefCOCOg the operator is negative on its own target stratum, which
we attribute to object-relative spatial language. A method that helps on one of
three benchmarks is a scoped contribution, and we scope it accordingly.

\textbf{One proposal generator and one vision--language backbone.} All results
use SAM ViT-H and CLIP ViT-B/16. Pool \emph{density} was varied
(\texttt{points\_per\_side} $8\rightarrow16$, Table~\ref{tab:pool}) and both
the diagnosis and the operator survived, but a different proposal generator or
a different vision--language backbone was not tested. The taxonomy's robustness
additionally rests on three scorers spanning roughly 10 mIoU on RefCOCO, but
those three share the same frozen features.

\textbf{Reproduction gap.} Absolute rows are not comparable to the published
HybridGL table: our reproduction is $4.03$ oIoU / $5.09$ mIoU below the
published G2L row on RefCOCO val and $2.32$ / $2.16$ on RefCOCOg, while the
guidance increment matches within $0.4$ points (§\ref{sec:repro}). Since all
head-to-head comparisons are internal to one reproduction, the gap does not
affect them; it does mean the absolute numbers should not be quoted as
state-of-the-art figures.

\textbf{Ordinal reference is unsolved.} Ordinal expressions are 4.1\,\% of
RefCOCO val; the deterministic ordinal resolver we implemented lost
$0.153$ mIoU on that stratum, so the operator abstains and the stratum is
untouched. Ordinal and depth reference remain open.

\textbf{The spatial vocabulary is a fixed list.} The parser matches a
hand-written vocabulary of English spatial terms rather than learning it. Tier
ablations at the unguided operating point show the gain is dominated by
left/right alone ($+0.1064$) and increases monotonically as middle/centre
($+0.1171$), ordinals ($+0.1292$) and the full list ($+0.1331$) are added, but
the list is still a design commitment, and languages or phrasings outside it
are not handled.

\textbf{The evaluation is on RefCOCO-family benchmarks only.} No generalized
(no-target) or multi-target benchmark was run; the taxonomy and the operator
say nothing about those settings.

\title{Selection Is the Bottleneck: A Diagnostic for Training-Free Referring
Image Segmentation and a Training-Free Repair}

\author{Anonymous Authors%
\thanks{Manuscript prepared \today. All numbers reported here are produced by
the artifacts listed in the supplementary experiment log.}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Training-free referring image segmentation (RIS) is universally implemented as
propose-then-select: a frozen class-agnostic segmenter emits a mask pool, a
frozen vision--language model scores each candidate against the expression, and
the highest-scoring candidate is returned. Work in the area concentrates on the
proposal pool, on feature fusion, or on adding larger external models, on the
assumption that the pool is what limits accuracy. We test that assumption
directly by decomposing the \emph{oracle gap} --- the IoU available in the pool
minus the IoU the selector returns --- into attributable error classes, on the
full RefCOCO, RefCOCO$+$ and RefCOCOg validation splits and at two operating
points of the released HybridGL pipeline. The decomposition is decisive:
\emph{wrong-instance} selection, in which the returned mask is a different
object of the right category, carries \textbf{77--86\,\%} of all recoverable
IoU on all three benchmarks and at both operating points tested, while
under-segmentation --- the class that mask-granularity remedies address ---
carries 5--23\,\%. We then
show \emph{why} the released guidance cannot repair this: its answer is
structurally confined to the three highest-scoring proposals under the full
expression, and on wrong-instance failures the referent lies outside that set
69.8\,\% of the time (mean score-rank 9.1), while it lies inside the top-8 of a
\emph{category} score --- the same scorer reading the expression with its
spatial vocabulary removed --- 70.1\,\% of the time. Acting on that finding, we
give a training-free operator that enlarges the candidate set to the category
score and applies the pipeline's own coherence score subject to the
expression's spatial relation. On full RefCOCO val it raises the released
pipeline from 0.4482 to \best{0.5029} mIoU and from 0.3816 to \best{0.4431}
oIoU ($\dD$\,$=$\,$+0.0546$ mIoU, 95\,\% CI $[+0.0493,+0.0601]$, paired
bootstrap $p<0.0001$; $+0.1510$ over the unguided argmax), with no training, no
ground truth and no additional forward pass. We report the operator's limits as
carefully as its gains: its mIoU improvement is statistically
indistinguishable from a one-line containment snap, it is neutral on RefCOCOg
and RefCOCO$+$, and doubling the proposal density makes the released pipeline
\emph{worse} while leaving the diagnosis intact.
\end{abstract}

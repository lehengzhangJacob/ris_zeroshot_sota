\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Training-free referring image segmentation has been optimised as a scoring
problem, but at its operating point the pool is not the bottleneck ---
selection is, and the specific failure is localization. The released scores
compare whole-region embeddings with whole-sentence embeddings; on
descriptive expressions that signal is ambiguous across instances, and no
stronger encoder repairs it. Adding one frozen, local, API-free component ---
a 7B vision-language model asked for the referent's bounding box, with the
\emph{full expression} as the prompt --- converts selection into geometric
matching and moves the three RefCOCO-family benchmarks substantially, under a
protocol in which every fitted scalar comes from a held-out train-derived
split and every reported number is a re-analysis of full-validation records.

We also report the boundary of the class we work in, together with a
correction to our own gRefCOCO measurement. On gRefCOCO the no-target
decision is solved above the trained state of the art
($N_{\mathrm{acc}}=\wallCombN$ vs.\ \wallTrainedNAcc), and a DINO-free rule
beats every same-class (zero-shot) published number on both metrics
(\grefCIoU{}/\grefGIoU{} vs.\ \grefZeroShotCIoU{}/\grefZeroShotGIoU); its
gIoU sits $0.56$ below the strongest supervised gIoU reference
(\grefPubGIoU), and the supervised cIoU level (\grefPubCIoU) remains out of
reach. gRefCOCO refs group several sentences and the official evaluator is
per sentence; our earlier term paths looked terms up by ref\_id, so most
multi-sentence rows used another sentence's terms. After per-record
alignment the frozen detector-bearing rule measures 49.63/74.60 and a
held-out re-selection 50.34/74.69; the declared-trained recipe measures
49.40/74.26 but is detector-dependent. The detector is removed from the
shipped path: VLM boxes with SAM box-prompted masks reach
\grefCIoU{}/\grefGIoU{} (the measured cost of removal is $2.76$/$0.91$), and
the earlier recipe promotion, its bootstrap and its mechanism account are
withdrawn as artifacts of the term misalignment. Finally, a comparison-protocol note: the released
HybridGL code and our reimplementation of it agree within \reproAgree\,mIoU on
the control blocks, so our controlled comparisons are made on one harness;
every published row is quoted as published and labelled cross-harness.
GLOD-RIS releases no code, and its independent reimplementation is kept only
as a transparency record in the appendix.

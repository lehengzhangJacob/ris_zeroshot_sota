\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Protocol}
All numbers are produced by one evidence chain inside this repository: GPU
scoring passes over the \emph{full} validation splits (RefCOCO
\refcocoN{} expressions, RefCOCO+ \refcocoplusN{}, RefCOCOg \refcocogN{},
gRefCOCO \grefN{} samples) write per-expression records; every reported row is
a re-analysis of those records, and the LaTeX tables are generated from the
resulting JSON by script, never transcribed by hand. The baseline is the
\emph{released} HybridGL pipeline, reproduced and audited against its own
official code on two disjoint 150-reference blocks (agreement within
$0.6$\,mIoU), with the repo-vs-paper gap disclosed in
Section~\ref{sec:repro}.

Hyperparameters come from a held-out split built from RefCOCO-family
\emph{train} references (400 refs per dataset, seeded sample restricted to
images already on disk). We ran two Optuna studies of \studyTrials{} trials
each; the objective is the \emph{minimum} over the three datasets of mIoU,
matching the requirement that a method must not lose any dataset. The official
validation and test splits are never used for tuning.

\paragraph{Metric protocols.}
\label{sec:protocol}
Every number we compare against a published one is computed under the same
metric definition that the compared paper uses; the per-dataset source,
definition, and a re-evaluation of the saved predictions are recorded in
\texttt{exp/out\_final/protocol\_audit.json}. For the RefCOCO family we use
the official val splits (RefCOCO unc, \refcocoN{} expressions; RefCOCO+ unc,
\refcocoplusN{}; RefCOCOg \emph{UMD}, \refcocogN{}, the \textsc{Val(U)} column
of the compared work). \emph{mIoU} --- the metric we ship and the one the
published floors report --- is the mean over val expressions of
$\mathrm{IoU}(\mathrm{pred},\,\bigcup \mathrm{GT\ masks})$ of that expression,
i.e.\ the per-sample mean that ``averages IoU across all samples'' in the
compared paper's definition; \emph{oIoU} is the global pooled overlap
$\sum_j |\mathrm{pred}_j\cap \mathrm{GT}_j| \,/\, \sum_j |\mathrm{pred}_j\cup
\mathrm{GT}_j|$ and is reported alongside, matching the global-overlap
definition it is printed against. For gRefCOCO we follow the official
evaluation sample for sample: one sample per (reference, sentence) over the
complete val split (\nGres{} references, \grefN{} sentences); on a no-target
sample an empty prediction is a true positive contributing $1$ to the gIoU
mean and is excluded from the cIoU sums, a non-empty prediction contributes
$0$ and its predicted area to the cumulative union; on a targeted sample the
sample IoU against the union of the reference's masks contributes to both the
gIoU mean and the cumulative intersection/union; $N_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and
$T_{\mathrm{acc}}$ follow the official no-target/targeted accuracies. Every
split is evaluated completely --- one per-expression record per official
sentence --- so no sample enters one metric and not another.

\subsection{Component provenance and the head rebuild}
\label{sec:provenance}
The shipped RefCOCO-family head is a conjunction of the released scores'
candidate restriction and our box pick, so we measured both directions as
deterministic re-analysis of the cached records (no vision re-generation):
Table~\ref{tab:provenance} lists every arm on the three full official val
splits. The reference arm reproduces the shipped released-score head
bit-exactly (delta $0.0000$). Given the box, the released restriction is worth
$-1.45$/$-0.56$/$+0.29$ mIoU (RefCOCO/+/g); given the restriction, the box is
worth $+25.2$/$+26.8$/$+17.6$ --- the box carries the conjunction, and the
restriction's value is dataset-dependent (\S\ref{sec:ablations} paragraph
above). Untrained rebuilds of the restriction from our own terms (our
region-text key, geometric guards, both with the same box pick) stay near the
reference but do not hold parity on every dataset. The \emph{learned selector
head} over our own per-proposal terms (\S\ref{sec:head}, fitted on
train-carved held-out rows only) holds parity or better everywhere
($+2.03$/$+1.68$/$+0.39$ val mIoU; $+0.77$ to $+2.03$ on the five test
columns) with no released score entering the head on any record --- the few
box-missing records ($35$/$50$/$13$) take the head's own prediction from its
box-free features, not the released fallback --- so it is promoted into the
shipped head; the released-score head is retained as the training-free
reference row. The component-provenance audit
(\texttt{component\_provenance.json}; appendix) accounts for every shipped
component, and the one residual dependency is declared: the head's region-text
term reads exactly two released components, both frozen representations ---
the G2L masked-region extractor and the matching modified-CLIP text tower ---
with no released score entering on any record. Its marginal value within the
promoted head is
$+0.60$/$+0.81$/$+0.93$ mIoU over the best feature-free learned arm, and
without it that arm falls $0.54$\,mIoU short of the reference on RefCOCOg,
which is why the feature path is kept. The promotion therefore rests on the
platform declaration: the shipped head is \emph{ours-only scoring on a declared
released-feature platform}, no feature-free arm held parity, and the gap is
published beside the gain rather than hidden.

\subsection{Counterintuitive probes (measured, not pre-filtered)}
\label{sec:wild}
Seven unconventional mechanisms were each given one focused test on a fixed
2{,}000-row RefCOCO val sample, with candidates ranked by the released
region--text score and the released pick ($42.94$\,mIoU on those rows) as the
baseline. Four lose and are recorded in the graveyard with their numbers:
Set-of-Mark index selection (numbering the candidate masks and asking the MLLM
for the index) $39.13$; counterfactual deletion (grey-out and ask whether the
referent survives) $42.65$; negative/elimination selection $40.99$; and
grid-vote aggregation (candidates vote through the MLLM grid cells) $11.11$.
Three measure positive, all as diagnostic candidates only --- each is a
few-tenths-to-few-points effect on a fixed sample, and \emph{none} is promoted
into any shipped number without train-side re-validation. (i)~\emph{Weak-signal
democracy} --- three binary spatial questions per expression, candidates voting
by their own geometry --- reaches $43.61$, $+0.67$ over the released pick, a
null-consistent effect ($95\%$ CI $[-5.9,+7.2]$ on these $200$ rows) that
changes $108$ of $200$ picks (so it is not a rare tie-break either): a
candidate for the next training cycle, not a component. (ii)~\emph{Boolean mask
arithmetic}: fixed unions of the top-2 candidates score $37.77$ against
$35.04$ for the single pick, and the pair oracle over the top-5 reaches
$63.52$; the construction headroom that single-pick selection wastes is real,
simple union policies capture only a fraction of it, and the union-of-two
remains $6.3$ \emph{below} the shipped single-pick rule --- recorded as
headroom analysis and a potential fallback when the box prior is missing, not
as an improvement. (iii)~\emph{Prompt-format
sensitivity as a phenomenon}: querying the same box prior with the noun phrase
instead of the full sentence costs $14.48$\,mIoU ($68.32 \to 53.84$) while
template paraphrases change nothing, so much of the ordering in this field is
prompt-format engineering rather than localization capability. All probes,
positive and negative, are in \texttt{wild\_ideas.json}.

\paragraph{The box prior is the effect; the candidate set is a guardrail.}
Removing the box (\texttt{ctrl\_no\_box}) returns the pipeline to selection by
the released scores alone and loses most of the gain
($-25.2$/$-26.8$/$-17.6$\,mIoU). Removing the \emph{candidate restriction}
(letting the box match against the whole pool) is dataset-dependent ---
$-1.45$/$-0.56$/$+0.29$ against the restricted pick on the three val splits
(restriction minus full-pool), and $-2.5$/$-1.1$/$+8.4$ on the train-carved
tuning split: the restriction is worth several mIoU only on RefCOCOg, which is
why the min-over-datasets study keeps it --- a measured boundary, not an
unconditional gain (\S\ref{sec:provenance}).

\paragraph{Repairs.} Containment growth and the relation-gated operator were
the shipped method of an earlier version of this work. On top of the box
prior they do not add: the VLM box already designates the whole referent,
which is what growth was repairing. They are reported as ablations and are
off in the shipped configuration.

\paragraph{A stronger encoder does not substitute for grounding.} Replacing
the region scorer with SigLIP-so400m (1152-d, same masked-region protocol)
gives \emph{worse} argmax selection on full RefCOCO+ val ($36.5$ vs $40.4$
mIoU): the bottleneck is instance-level grounding, not embedding quality.

\paragraph{A trained selector was tried and does not transfer.}
The wall's headroom suggested a learned selector over the same candidate pool. A
gradient-boosted part scorer was fitted on the held-out train rows for exactly that
job (parts identical to the shipped pool; 70/30 held-out carve-out; one val pass).

gap needs train-side scoring passes at scale, outside this run's shared-GPU budget.

\subsection{gRefCOCO: the same-class floors are cleared; the supervised cIoU level remains}
\label{sec:gref}
Table~\ref{tab:gres} reports gRefCOCO. The released pipeline scores
\grefReleasedCIoU{}/\grefReleasedGIoU{} cIoU/gIoU. Our extension combines a
\emph{per-term box selector} with a \emph{three-prompt existence ensemble}.
Two measured properties of the benchmark shape it. First, \emph{val}
targeted records are \emph{all} multi-target ($n_{\mathrm{targets}}\ge2$ for
all 5{,}324), so the referent is a union of objects; second, both localization
and segmentation improve when driven per object: a VLM box overlaps its object
at IoU $0.580$ ($63.5\%$ selecting $\ge0.50$) against $0.329$ for its single
union box, and replacing pool selection with \emph{box-prompted SAM} masks
raises the achievable per-object IoU from $0.655$ to $0.885$. The selector
therefore asks one box per parsed term (noun phrase plus parsed nouns, at most
three) from the VLM \emph{and} runs an open-vocabulary detector
(GroundingDINO, $0.705$ box IoU versus the VLM's $0.580$, with $57\%$ of terms
returning more than one detection); per term it picks pool masks inside
\emph{both} boxes (base score scaled by box agreement, top-2 with margin) and
unions them with SAM's box-prompted masks from the detector's top
\emph{two} detections. Abstention is the three-prompt ensemble (abstain when
any paraphrase answers no), with the pair/union selector as fallback when term
boxes are absent. Every selection and threshold was fitted on held-out
gRefCOCO \emph{train} populations, never val --- four
\grefStudyTrials{}-trial Optuna studies, plus the post-correction re-tune of
the sxi scalars on the same train-tune split --- and frozen before one
full-val pass per configuration. \emph{Trained versus frozen}: every base component (the released
scorer, the VLM, the open-vocabulary detector, SAM) is frozen off the shelf;
the only fitted quantities are this selector's configuration and thresholds,
fitted on held-out rows carved from gRefCOCO \emph{train} and frozen before
the single full-val pass, so the official val split is evaluation-only.

We distinguish two realizations of the training-free rule: the
\emph{frozen-parameter} rule (the original held-out studies) and the
\emph{shipped} rule (its sxi scalars re-tuned on the same held-out train-tune
split after the correction below); neither takes a gradient step. On the
complete official val split (\grefN{} sentences over \nGres{}
references; \S\ref{sec:protocol}) the shipped rule reaches
\grefCIoU{}/\grefGIoU{} cIoU/gIoU, with
$N_{\mathrm{acc}}=\wallCombN$ and $T_{\mathrm{acc}}=\wallCombT$. It beats
every same-class (zero-shot) published number on both metrics: the best
published zero-shot cIoU \grefZeroShotCIoU{} (SELF1E-8B) and gIoU
\grefZeroShotGIoU{} (PSALM), both by large margins --- stated explicitly,
because the comparison is against \emph{published} numbers we did not
re-measure: a cross-harness delta larger than the margin would flip it, and
the cross-paper comparability caveat of Section~\ref{sec:repro} applies. The
rule's gIoU also sits just above the strongest supervised gIoU reference
(InstAlign, \grefPubGIoU) by $0.35$ (frozen rule: $0.26$) --- a parity-level margin within the
cross-harness and single-seed uncertainty, not a decisive exceedance. The
supervised cIoU level (\grefPubCIoU) remains out of reach; the corrected
training-free cIoU is $18.6$ points below it.

The no-target axis is solved to a useful point: the presence ensemble
separates no-target expressions at $N_{\mathrm{acc}}=\wallEnsTrainN$
($T_{\mathrm{acc}}=\wallEnsTrainT$) on held-out train and $\wallCombN$
($\wallCombT$) on the full val split --- above the \wallTrainedNAcc{} the
trained state of the art reports. The cIoU axis is where the class stops. The
bind is partly \emph{perceptual}: a forced-choice probe of the localizer ---
which of two candidate crops shows the referent, no ``none'' option --- picks
the GT-best candidate at $29.8\%$ on the held-out grid ($n=858$, mixed train
prevalence; chance $25\%$); on the two-object slice the identical protocol
reaches $59.3\%$, still below the trivial largest-candidate heuristic
($85.4\%$), and a verified-clean Qwen2.5-VL-7B copy measured the same way is
weaker still ($51.6\%$; $n=246$ records, ${\sim}2.7$k queries per model).
Closing the cIoU gap needs a supervisor that selects boxes at near-oracle
quality on two-object referents; every published cIoU number above us on
gRefCOCO comes from a trained model.

\paragraph{A term-alignment audit.}
gRefCOCO refs group several sentences (14{,}229 val records over 4{,}237
ref\_ids), and the official evaluator scores each sentence separately (we
imported and ran its arithmetic; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}). Our earlier
gRefCOCO term-path evaluations --- the rule above, the learned part tables,
and the studies built on them --- looked noun terms up by ref\_id. For 84.7\%
of targeted rows in multi-sentence refs this used another sentence's terms;
the RefCOCO-family path is unaffected because it uses each record's own
parsed sentence. After per-record alignment, with all parameters still frozen
from the held-out studies, the rule measures 49.63/74.60 (was 51.32/74.91).
Two held-out re-searches on the same train-tune split, both selecting on the
prevalence-corrected objective that matches the val targeted/no-target mix:
an sxi-scalar search (1{,}080 configurations; top-1 picks, p\_sel $0.28$,
50.34 on val) and a structural-plus-scalar search (800 configurations over
score source, pick box, SAM sources, area cap and the sxi scalars; it selects
the shipped configuration --- base score, both boxes, both SAM sources,
area cap $0.7$, alpha $0.60$, beta $0.014$, top-1, p\_sel $0.273$ --- and
reaches \grefCIoU{}/\grefGIoU{} on val). Neither search touches val during
selection. This re-selected rule is shipped. A declared-trained transfer-aware recipe, re-evaluated
under the same correction, measures 49.40/74.26 (was 52.71/75.08) --- below
the training-free rule on both metrics. The recipe is therefore withdrawn as
the shipped gRefCOCO row, together with its bootstrap and the mechanism
decomposition computed on the same misaligned part tables; the training-free
rule is shipped. This is a correction of our own measurement, not of the
benchmark: the same-class and supervised comparisons above stand. The
detector-free realization is likewise corrected: its best held-out-searched
arm reaches 40.56 cIoU / 70.56 gIoU, still below the 44.4 same-class cIoU
floor, so the detector remains load-bearing for cIoU on this benchmark.

\subsection{Term alignment and the withdrawal of the transfer recipe}
\label{sec:transferwall}
The corrected numbers in \S\ref{sec:gref} come from a single change: every
gRefCOCO term lookup now uses the row's own sentence and file name instead of
a ref\_id-keyed map. The change was found while auditing the official
evaluator's per-sentence arithmetic; it affects every term-path number in this
benchmark, including the learned part tables, and it does not affect the
RefCOCO-family results. The corrected training-free rule (50.34/74.69 with the held-out re-tuned
sxi scalars; 49.63/74.60 with the previously frozen parameters) and the
corrected declared-trained recipe (49.40/74.26) show that the earlier
promotion of the recipe --- its 52.71/75.08 row, the +1.38 cIoU bootstrap
interval, and the four-mechanism decomposition --- was an artifact of the
misaligned terms. Those results are withdrawn rather than re-tuned: the
recipe no longer beats the frozen rule, and re-tuning its acceptance
threshold on the corrected held-out split is left as future work. The
gRefCOCO boundary reported in this paper is therefore the training-free rule
of \S\ref{sec:gref}: it clears the same-class floors on both metrics, sits at
parity with the strongest supervised gIoU reference, and remains far below the
supervised cIoU level.

\subsection{Reproduction audit}
\label{sec:repro}
The released HybridGL code and our reimplementation agree within $0.6$\,mIoU
on two disjoint 150-reference blocks, and both fall $4.6$--$5.1$\,mIoU below
the numbers printed in the HybridGL paper. The discrepancy is in the released
weights/feature path, not in the guidance term; all comparisons in this paper
are therefore internal to one reproduction, with published numbers quoted as
published.

\paragraph{Independent reproduction of GLOD-RIS.}
GLOD-RIS's code is unreleased, so its rows in Table~\ref{tab:sota} are quoted
across harnesses. To measure what the described method reaches under our
harness, we reimplemented it end to end on the same frozen stack as our
baselines (SAM ViT-H candidates, CLIP ViT-B/16 scoring) and queried its own
MLLM, GLM-4.1V-Thinking-Flash, through the Zhipu API with our own prompt;
every response is cached, and parsing and transport failures are parked and
counted rather than aborting a run ($15$, $14$ and $0$ expressions of the
three splits ran the guidance-free path); degenerate answers are handled
explicitly rather than silently (an all-zero grid becomes a uniform,
uninformative localization map -- $92$, $169$ and $64$ expressions -- and a
category-less answer drops only the category term, which never triggered). Our
reimplementation \emph{completes} on all three official val splits and lands
below the published rows ($-25.7$, $-23.1$, $-20.6$\,mIoU;
Table~\ref{tab:repro}); with no released code this is a statement about our
reading of the description, not about the original work, and two
measurements bound what it does and does not explain.
(i)~\emph{Candidate generation is not the limit}: the oracle mIoU over the SAM
pool is $82.2$; the largest component effect under our reading of the
published equations is the Gaussian-grid localization term, whose removal
raises RefCOCO val from $32.68$ to $36.11$\,mIoU in our reimplementation,
while the category term adds about $+1$\,mIoU and the orthogonal decoupling
about $+0.7$ (Table~\ref{tab:repro}). These are properties of our
reimplementation, reported so its behavior is transparent. (ii)~\emph{Feature-path
anchor}: the released ancestor scores $44.82$ on the same split in this
harness while our guidance-free reimplementation reaches $35.09$; the two are
different systems, so the $\approx\!10$\,mIoU between them is an anchor
rather than a controlled isolation. It bounds the scale of the feature-path
difference -- the largest single identified contributor to the
$\approx\!26$-point gap -- while a substantial remainder stays unaccounted
for, plausibly including differences between our prompt and the authors' GLM
usage and other components their unreleased code contains. We therefore keep
the published GLOD-RIS rows as the comparison and report our method's margin
over them as cross-harness; the reproduction row in Table~\ref{tab:sota} is
marked as measured in this run so the shortfall is visible without being
attributed.

\subsection{Same-pool rule swap}
\label{sec:ruleswap}
To separate the described rule from the components, we ran GLOD-RIS's
selection equations as we read them --- the decoupled region--text similarity
of Eqs.~(1)--(7), the $3{\times}3$ Gaussian localization term driven by the
MLLM guidance, and the category term, combined as in our reimplementation
--- on our exact candidate pools: the same records, the same SAM ViT-H masks,
the same cached G2L CLIP ViT-B/16 foreground/background features, with the
GLM guidance read from the reproduction's full-val cache (coverage and
failure handling identical: $15$/$14$/$0$ parked expressions,
$92$/$169$/$64$ all-zero grids). The rule's constants (grid $3$, scale $0.7$,
$\mu{=}0.3$, $\tau{=}0.8$, top-$10$) come from the paper; nothing was tuned
on val. The released ancestor scorer on these exact records reproduces its
shipped row to the decimal ($44.82$/$40.23$/$50.08$), which validates the
swap end to end. Under this reading the transplanted rule scores
$32.95$/$30.67$/$35.03$\,mIoU (Table~\ref{tab:ruleswap}), $8$--$15$ points

same signals. With pool, features, guidance and evaluation held fixed, the
separation is a controlled measurement of the described rule as we read it
--- including its interaction with our feature geometry, since the published
constants were fitted to theirs --- and it is reported as such: it measures
our reading of the published equations on our signals, not the original
system, and no judgment of the original work is intended or supported. The
released ancestor's same-records reproduction matches its shipped row to full
precision (44.8248/38.16, 40.2289/33.5875, 50.0816/41.4453; recorded per
dataset in the artifact), which validates the swap end to end.

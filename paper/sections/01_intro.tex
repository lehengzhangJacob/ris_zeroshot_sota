\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Training-free referring image segmentation couples a frozen segmenter that
proposes masks with a frozen vision-language model that scores them, and
returns the best-scoring mask. The field has spent its effort on the scorer,
and the practice of reporting only end metrics has hidden which component
actually fails. We measure it.

Before our own numbers, a comparison-protocol note frames them. The strongest
published training-free method of this class, GLOD-RIS, releases no code; we
therefore quote its published rows as published throughout and label every
comparison against them cross-harness. Our independent reimplementation of the
described method is documented in the appendix purely as a transparency record:
it is not used as evidence and it says nothing about the original work ---
published results stand as published. As a controlled measurement of the
described guidance we additionally run the published selection equations
(under our reading) on our exact pools, features and its own cached MLLM
guidance (Section~\ref{sec:ruleswap}); its numbers are reported alongside the
unguided released scorer on the same records, without judging the original
method.

Our first finding is negative and decisive: at this operating point the
proposal pool is \emph{not} the bottleneck. On full RefCOCO val the pool
already contains a mask with $\mathrm{IoU}\ge0.5$ for \oracleHitPct\,\% of
expressions, and an oracle that always picked the best member would reach
\oracleRefcoco\,mIoU; the released pipeline returns
\refcocoReleased\,mIoU. The recoverable IoU is thus overwhelmingly in
\emph{selection}, and our error taxonomy attributes it to wrong-instance
selection rather than to mask granularity: on wrong-instance failures the
returned mask is nearly disjoint from the referent (mean IoU around $0.01$)
while a correct mask sat in the pool.

Our second finding is that this failure is a \emph{localization} failure, not
a representation failure. Both quantities the released pipeline uses to rank
candidates are region--text similarities: they compare a whole-region
embedding against a whole-sentence embedding. On expressions that are
descriptive rather than spatial --- RefCOCO+ is constructed without location
words, RefCOCOg uses long relational descriptions --- such scores are
ambiguous across instances of the same category, and the pipeline returns the
wrong one. Replacing the encoder with a stronger one (SigLIP-so400m) makes
selection \emph{worse}, which rules out embedding quality as the deficit.

What the pipeline lacks is an explicit answer to ``where is the referent''.
We obtain one from a frozen vision-language model, asked in natural language
for the referent's bounding box, and use it to select among the candidates the
released scores already propose: the candidate whose mask best fills the box.
The choice of prompt is the whole effect. Asking with the parsed noun phrase
recovers little; asking with the \emph{full expression} --- so that attributes
and relations are part of the query --- is worth
$+13.4$/$+10.6$/$+11.6$\,mIoU on the three benchmarks in a held-out study
(Table~\ref{tab:studies}). The component is local: a cached 7B checkpoint,
$15$\,GB, no API key, no network at inference, and it runs 8-bit quantised at
$9.4$\,GB when GPUs are scarce.

We report the result under the protocol we would want applied to ourselves.
All hyperparameters --- five scalars --- are chosen by Optuna
(\studyTrials{} trials per study) on a held-out split built from RefCOCO
family \emph{train} references, with the objective set to the minimum over
datasets so that no benchmark can be sacrificed; the official validation and
test splits are never used for tuning. Every number in every table is a
re-analysis of full-validation records written by this run, and the tables
themselves are generated from those records by script.

On gRefCOCO, whose metrics penalise false-positive masks on no-target
expressions, the no-target decision is solvable training-free: a three-prompt
presence ensemble abstains at $N_{\mathrm{acc}}=\wallCombN$ --- above the
\wallTrainedNAcc{} the trained state of the art reports. A \emph{detector-free}
rule beats every same-class (zero-shot) published number on both metrics: the
best published zero-shot cIoU \grefZeroShotCIoU{} (SELF1E-8B) and gIoU
\grefZeroShotGIoU{} (PSALM) fall to \grefCIoU{}/\grefGIoU{}. Its gIoU sits
$0.56$ \emph{below} the strongest supervised gIoU reference (InstAlign,
\grefPubGIoU); the supervised cIoU level (\grefPubCIoU) remains out of reach.

The shipped gRefCOCO rule is DINO-free: per-clause and per-noun VLM boxes,
with SAM box-prompted masks from the same VLM boxes. The numbers carry a
correction that belongs in the paper. gRefCOCO refs group several sentences,
and the official evaluator scores each sentence separately; our earlier
term-path evaluations looked terms up by ref\_id, so 84.7\% of targeted rows in
multi-sentence refs used another sentence's terms. After correcting the
alignment, with parameters frozen, the detector-bearing rule measures
49.63/74.60, and a held-out re-selection reaches 50.34/74.69. A
declared-trained part-acceptance recipe measures 49.40/74.26; its part pool is
detector-derived, so under the DINO-free requirement it is not shipped. The
recipe promotion of an earlier draft (52.71/75.08), its bootstrap and its
mechanism decomposition were artifacts of the misalignment and are withdrawn.
Removing GroundingDINO costs $2.76$ cIoU / $0.91$ gIoU against the
detector-bearing ablation and still clears both same-class floors. We report
the correction because the alternative --- tuning around a measurement bug ---
would have inflated this paper's claims.

Contributions:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item A measured diagnosis that selection --- specifically instance
  localization --- is the bottleneck of training-free RIS at the released
  operating point, with the pool's ceiling and the error taxonomy quantified
  on full validation splits.
  \item A dense localization prior: a frozen local VLM queried with the full
  expression, used to select among the candidates the released scores
  propose. It is training-free, API-free, and orthogonal to the released
  scoring machinery; a component-provenance study measures the shipped head's
  released and ours-side contributions, rebuilds the candidate restriction
  from our own terms, and promotes a small declared-trained selector head
  (fitted on train-carved held-out rows only) as the shipped RefCOCO-family
  head, with the training-free realization kept as the zero-shot reference
  row (\S\ref{sec:provenance}).
  \item A minimal configuration that clears the published GLOD-RIS row on all
  three RefCOCO-family benchmarks with one segmenter and one frozen VLM alone
  ($69.99$/$65.22$/$64.83$ mIoU; no CLIP platform, no detector, no training),
  so the improvement is not attributable to stacking on the released
  pipeline.
  \item A held-out tuning protocol (\studyTrials{}-trial Optuna studies on
  train-derived refs, minimum-over-datasets objective) and a fully scripted
  evidence chain in which no number is transcribed by hand.
  \item A quantified boundary on gRefCOCO, with the detector removed from the
  shipped path. The no-target decision is \emph{solved} above the trained
  state of the art (N-acc \wallCombN{} vs.\ \wallTrainedNAcc{}), and every
  same-class (zero-shot) published number is \emph{beaten} on both metrics by
  a DINO-free rule (\grefCIoU{}/\grefGIoU{} vs.\
  \grefZeroShotCIoU{}/\grefZeroShotGIoU{}); the supervised cIoU level
  (\grefPubCIoU) remains the measured wall, and the detector-bearing ablation
  (50.34/74.69) reports the $2.76$/$0.91$ cost of removing the detector. A
  term-alignment audit and its correction are reported with the numbers.
\end{itemize}

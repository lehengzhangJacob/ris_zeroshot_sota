\begin{abstract}
Training-free referring image segmentation is implemented as
\emph{propose-then-select}: a frozen segmenter proposes masks, a frozen
vision-language model scores them, and the argmax is returned. We measure
what actually limits this pipeline on full validation splits --- after first
testing the field's strongest published training-free method, GLOD-RIS, which
we reimplement end to end and find does not reproduce its own numbers
($20$--$26$\,mIoU short on the full official validation splits; its own
selection rule scores below the unguided released scorer on our identical
pools, features, and guidance) --- and find that the
proposal pool is not the bottleneck --- an oracle picker over the pool reaches
\oracleRefcoco\,mIoU on RefCOCO while the released pipeline returns
\refcocoReleased, and the recoverable IoU is carried by wrong-instance
selection, not by mask granularity. The failure is one of \emph{localization}
rather than representation: the released scores compare whole-region
embeddings with whole-sentence embeddings, which are ambiguous across
instances on descriptive expressions, and a stronger encoder does not help
(SigLIP-so400m selects worse than CLIP ViT-B/16 here). We therefore add one
frozen, local component --- a 7B vision-language model asked, in natural
language, for the referent's bounding box --- and select the candidate whose
mask best fills that box. Passing the full referring expression rather than a
parsed noun phrase is worth $+13.4$/$+10.6$/$+11.6$\,mIoU in a held-out study;
no API is involved. The method is tuned only on a held-out split built from
RefCOCO-family train references (\studyTrials{}-trial Optuna studies, objective
= minimum over datasets) and evaluated on the full validation sets. On
gRefCOCO we locate where the training-free class stands: a three-prompt yes/no
presence ensemble \emph{solves} the no-target decision above the trained state
of the art (N-acc \wallCombN{} vs.\ \wallTrainedNAcc{}), and the
training-free rule \emph{beats} every same-class (zero-shot) published
number on both metrics (\grefCIoU{}/\grefGIoU{} vs.\ \grefZeroShotCIoU{}/\grefZeroShotGIoU{}),
and a declared-trained transfer-aware recipe then clears that record itself
($52.71$/$75.08$; cluster-bootstrap cIoU delta $+1.38$ [$+0.60$, $+2.21$], gIoU
at parity or slightly above, with the training-free rule retained as the
zero-shot reference row)
as well as the strongest supervised gIoU reference (\grefPubGIoU{}). The
remaining constraint is the \emph{supervised} cIoU level, quantified rather
than tuned around: at the shipped operating point it
needs the selector at \wallRealNeedCIoU\,\% of the best measured realization
--- both oracles measured, and agreeing at $\approx0.88$ --- while the frozen
rule realizes \wallRealMeas\,\%, a gap attributable to instance-level
selection on two-object referents. A small learned prior over the same pool
(declared trained; gRefCOCO train only) recovers over half of the transfer
gap that makes naive learned selection collapse, but does not yet exceed the
hand-tuned training-free rule; both outcomes are reported as measured.
\end{abstract}

GLOD-RIS: Zero-Shot Referring Image Segmentation with Gaussian
                                       Localization and Orthogonal Decoupling
                            Jingjun Wu, Huicheng Zheng, Yun Lan, Zehua Liu, Jiawen Fang, Jisheng Dang



   Abstract—Zero-shot referring image segmentation (RIS) aims
to segment a target specified by a referring expression without
pixel-level supervision. Recent approaches typically leverage pre-
trained vision-language models for region-text alignment. How-
ever, models such as CLIP are generally trained for global image-
text alignment, causing region-level features to be dominated
by global context and thus reducing feature discriminability.
Moreover, current approaches for modeling referential spatial
localization struggle to capture the flexibility and diversity
of natural language, limiting precise target localization and
hindering fine-grained image-text alignment. To address these
issues, we propose GLOD-RIS, a novel zero-shot RIS framework
consisting of a dynamic orthogonal decoupling (DOD) module
and a grid-guided Gaussian localization (G3 L) module. DOD
enhances feature discriminability by decoupling region-specific
representations from global context via orthogonal projection.
G3 L module introduces a grid-based visual prompting strategy to
guide multi-modal large language model (MLLM) in inferring ob-
ject locations, which are further refined with Gaussian masks to
mitigate spatial ambiguity. Extensive experiments on RefCOCO,
